Case study 01 · Encore ARC

Making complex operations work at scale.

A CRM transformation across Finance, Operations, Customer Service, and Legal teams.

FocusProduct execution
EnvironmentFinancial services

The context

As the business expanded from roughly 32,000 to more than 400,000 accounts, teams across Operations, Finance, Customer Service, and Legal relied on spreadsheet-heavy processes. Leadership assigned me to lead the CRM implementation because I understood the details of the existing workflows. My role was to connect business needs with a five-person external engineering team and guide the project through deployment.

Choosing what to build first

Every team had valid requests, but engineering capacity was finite. I weighed two questions: how many hours could automation save, and what would be the cost of leaving a workflow unchanged? I used these trade-offs to negotiate sequencing with stakeholders rather than prioritize by department or seniority.

01 · Customer letters

EMI and No Dues Certificate (NDC) letters came first because these documents were sent directly to customers.

02 · Finance ledgers

Manual customer-ledger preparation was labor-intensive. Automating it promised faster turnaround and meaningful time savings.

03 · Customer Service tracker

We prioritized complaint tracking and customer communication ahead of remaining legal workflow migration.

Deferred · Legal data

The Legal team could temporarily continue managing demand notice data in Excel while more urgent workflows were developed.

From workflows to production

  1. Documented current-state processes. Mapped how teams worked and identified manual steps and dependencies.
  2. Created BRDs. Translated the documented workflows into business requirements for the vendor.
  3. Agreed on delivery timelines. Worked with the five-person vendor engineering team to plan development based on requirements.
  4. Ran regular reviews. Followed up on progress, clarified requirements, and resolved blockers.
  5. Tested real scenarios. Validated features against multiple business cases, then coordinated user acceptance testing (UAT).
  6. Moved approved functionality to production. Completed testing before transferring the solution to the main server.

What this taught me

Prioritization isn't saying one team's work matters more. It's deciding what the business cannot afford to delay.

My biggest contribution was turning detailed process knowledge into clear requirements and defensible sequencing decisions, then staying involved until the workflows were ready for real users.

Outcome detail to verify: a turnaround-time reduction from three days to 24 hours was previously reported, but the exact workflow and measurement period need confirmation before publishing it as a quantified result.

Case study 02 · Urban Company

Scaling Quality Assurance with AI.

A human-in-the-loop image audit system across Men’s Grooming and Spa.

Adherence25% → 53%
Customer rating4.4 → 4.7

The challenge

Professionals photographed their setups at customers’ homes, but manual agents could only audit a limited share of the large photo volume. We needed a scalable way to identify missing required items without removing human judgment.

Designing the workflow

I worked with engineers to create image sets that enabled AI recognition of required setup items and helped classify requirements as essential or non-essential. AI-flagged cases were reviewed by human agents.

Essential items

Following a confirmed failure, the professional’s service calendar was blocked until they purchased the required item.

Non-essential items

Instead of blocking availability, we coached partners on setup improvements to support customer experience and tier progression.

Enabling adoption

We held multiple partner focus group discussions to explain the new checks, setup standards, and consequences. The severity-based response balanced enforcement with professional experience.

Observed results

Across Men’s Grooming and Spa combined, product adherence improved from 25% to 53% (+28 percentage points), and customer ratings improved from 4.4 to 4.7. These are category-level before-and-after outcomes, not an isolated estimate of AI’s causal effect.

AI helped scale detection; people and policy determined a proportionate response.

Case study 03 · RK Enterprises

Building a Dynamic Pricing Engine.

A cost-sensitive catalogue and downloadable rate-list tool for a traditional keychain business.

Catalogue654 varieties
PlatformBubble.io

The problem

My father’s keychain business offered 654 varieties. The annual catalogue was prepared with external help, while established selling prices were often revised using a percentage increase. This offered limited visibility into material, production, and other costs—or the profitability of individual products.

What I built

Using Bubble.io, I built a component-based pricing tool. When a raw material price changes, the system automatically recalculates prices for all affected keychains. My father can download an updated rate list for new institutional customers.

Centralized cost inputs

Update a material price once instead of revising affected products individually.

Profitability visibility

Break down material, production, other costs, and profit to see which products are commercially attractive.

Downloadable rate list

Prepare updated pricing for new institutional buyers.

Segment-aware adoption

Existing agents and intermediaries still prefer percentage-based increases to established prices.

Designing for a real user

My father’s early feedback was blunt: “We’ll talk when it’s usable.” The lesson was to focus on practical workflows rather than feature count. New institutional customers benefited from the cost-based approach; established intermediaries did not need the same change.

Next step

WhatsApp sharing integration is pending. The working product currently supports cost updates, automatic repricing, and rate-list downloads.

Case study 04 · Urban Company

Redesigning the Professional Toolkit.

Using partner research, extended testing, and sourcing strategy to improve everyday tools.

Partner product rating4.0 → 4.4
ApproachFGDs + longer testing

The initial objective

We began revamping fixed items—scissors, cutting capes, trimmers, and manicure and pedicure tools—with a focus on affordability. But professionals were not always buying the products we sold or using items in their initial kits.

What partner FGDs revealed

Uncompetitive prices

Some comparable tools were cheaper in the open market.

Rust and durability

A low purchase price was not viable if tools deteriorated quickly.

Scissor smoothness

Professionals needed smooth cutting action for reliable daily use.

Customer comfort

Quieter scissors could improve the in-service customer experience.

Turning insight into action

We searched for better-quality products at similar prices and adopted more extensive, longer-duration testing to evaluate performance beyond first impressions. To support affordability, we explored increasing order quantities for selected SKUs and consolidating vendors to negotiate better prices.

Observed result

Average partner ratings for the changed products improved from 4.0 to 4.4. The project showed how user research, validation, and procurement strategy can work together to improve physical products.

The goal shifted from finding the cheapest tool to finding one professionals would choose to use.
